If you want to sell a record collection in Nottingham, you do not need a long clear-out first. I am Steven Gee. I live in Bottesford, NG13, in the Vale of Belvoir. Pink Moon Records is just me. There is no shop, and this is not a business. I buy vinyl for my own shelves. I will come and see the records in the comfort of your own home.

A shelf in a spare room is a normal way for records to turn up. So is a run of boxes in the loft, or a pile in a house you are clearing. This note is about the small things that help when I visit, and about the jobs you can skip.

## Before you sell a record collection in Nottingham

There is no need to sort the collection, and no need to clean very much. I would rather see the records as they have been kept. You do not have to learn the names on the labels, or pull the rock out from the rest.

A rough count helps. A phone photo of the shelves helps too. If the records are in boxes, a photo of the boxes is just as useful. No full list is needed, because I can stand in the room and look.

If you pick a record up, hold it by the edges. A thumb across the playing surface can leave a mark. You do not have to take them out of the sleeves to show me. Singles can stay in their paper sleeves. Albums can stay in the outer sleeves. A box set can stay in its box.

## Keep them upright, and out of heat and damp

Records are happier standing upright, the way they sit on a shelf. If yours are already like that, leave them. A short lean is nothing to worry about. A flat pile, with something heavy on top, is harder on the sleeves and on the discs. If they have been stored flat for years, you do not have to restack every one before I come. Tell me, and leave them.

Heat and damp are worth avoiding, if you can do it without a large job. Full sun in a window, a radiator, a loft that gets very hot, or a cellar that feels wet are poor places for them to wait. A cool, dry room is enough. You do not need a special cabinet, and you do not need to buy anything.

If they are already packed from a move, leave them in the boxes. Stand the boxes so the records are upright inside, when that is easy. Do not unpack the lot onto the floor. I can look through a box where it sits. A crate or a cupboard that has been shut for a long time is fine too.

## Sleeves, inserts, posters and boxes

Keep the paper with the record it belongs to. Inner sleeves, lyric sheets, posters, booklets and the outer box of a box set should stay together. If a poster is folded inside a gatefold, leave it there. If an insert has fallen out and you know which sleeve it came from, put it back. If you are not sure, keep the loose paper in the same box. Do not throw it away because it looks like scrap.

Do not throw out a tatty sleeve. A split corner, a ring mark, or a spine gone soft from years on a shelf is still part of the record. Original UK pressings matter to me, and the sleeve is part of that. A plain, played, well-loved copy is welcome too. A tired sleeve is not a reason to part it from the disc.

Leave a gatefold closed. You do not need to flatten it, or tape a split. If the inner sleeve carries the picture or the words, it stays inside. The cardboard box the albums have lived in can stay with them. I would rather see the whole thing than a row of bare records.

## Please do not deep-clean them

Dust is nothing to apologise for. Please do not deep-clean a record with household products. Washing-up liquid, window spray, alcohol, or anything from under the sink can do more harm than years of playing. Do not wipe the playing surface with a tissue, a paper towel, or the sleeve of a shirt.

If a record looks dull, leave it dull. I will look at plain, played copies. You do not have to make them shine. Do not scrub the sleeve, and do not put sticky tape across a tear. Leave the wear where it is.

## Do not split the collection before I have seen it

Please leave the collection together until I have looked. I collect all kinds of music for my own shelves, not only the rock. Tell me about the whole collection, not only the records you think I want.

There is one clear exception. If you want to keep some, keep them, and say so. A few you still play, or a few that should stay in the family, can come off the shelf. Put them in another room, or name them in the email. Then I will look at the rest, and I will not have to guess.

Do not pull out only the famous names, and do not make a pile for the tip before I have been. Mixed boxes from the loft are welcome. A good record is never turned away. If you are not sure they are my sort, I would rather see them than have you decide for me.

## What I am hoping to see

Rock from the 1960s and 1970s is the music I have loved longest, and I mean any type of it: psychedelic, blues rock, hard rock, progressive, folk rock, heavy rock, early heavy metal, garage and freakbeat. The records wanted first are late 1960s and early 1970s rock. Albums, singles and box sets, in their sleeves if you still have them.

Original UK pressings, gatefold sleeves and the old label designs make me smile. Island pink label, Vertigo swirl, Harvest, Charisma, Deram, Atlantic plum, Parlophone and Immediate are names I like to see. You do not have to check each label. If you happen to notice one, you can say so. If not, I will see it when I come.

Played copies are welcome. A record that has been on a turntable for years is still a record I want to hold. The loud and the quiet, the famous records and the one that never left its sleeve.

I may be interested in more than that rock. I collect soul and Motown, country, folk, reggae, and mixed boxes from the loft. Any situation or quantity is always considered. I buy for my own shelves.
